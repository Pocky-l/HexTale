## [Unreleased]

### Added
- The Void has a branch of its own in the researcher's diary, apart from hextech and chemtech. It appears the first time
  the land around you shows the haze and follows what you do about it: let the land go and see what it turns into, and
  (in the plans still being written) hold a breach in place and heal the land around it. What happens on the way is
  written down as it happens - a mite latching on, a breach tearing open - each with the researcher's thoughts.
- The Void's steps look sick: dark cracked medallions with an angry magenta glow that flares out of rhythm, writhing
  thorns, dark smoke and a deep plum darkness around them with a smouldering magenta edge, veins with a throb instead of lines; the breach and the first sign of
  the haze wear a corona that swallows the light. Their names in the diary and in chat are in the Void's own ink, and a
  step of the Void done sounds of its own: a slow pen stroke over a dark breath.
- The diary points at what is new: an arrow on the edge of the page, or over the step when it is in view, for every
  step that just opened or was written down by itself because something happened (that one wears a turning gold ring).
  Each arrow tells where it leads by its look: a brass compass needle for the workshop, a falling drop for the
  essences, chevrons of rune ink for the runes, a hextech crystal with sparks for the altar circle, a pylon's claw on a
  tendril for the pylons, a red-hot spearhead trailing flame for the forge, a leaf on a vine for chemtech and a black
  thorn with an angry crack for the Void. Arrows of one branch that point the same way join into one with a count; a
  click on one flies the page to its step, and the arrow goes once the mouse has been over the step. Steps you finish
  yourself are never marked.
- A diary step can stand for a set of things: its icon switches between them, and its page shows a row of them to pick
  from.

### Changed
- The diary's sky is a researcher's star chart now: a deep night from blue to warm plum, two veils of nebula at their
  own depths, a band of milky light, great circles in faint gold ink with ticks turning slowly, meridians, a compass
  rose and the nine primal signs round the chart, small constellations, stars with crossed glints, a falling star now
  and then, warm motes of dust drifting up and a darker rim. The mouse leaves a short trail of star dust as it moves,
  and the stars near it shine a little brighter.
- A new Separator has no pipe fittings sticking out any more. A side opens by itself when something is set right
  beside it: a pipe or an alembic opens an inlet, a jar with essence in it an inlet too, an empty jar the concentrate
  outlet (a second empty jar the rest outlet). Take it away and the side shuts again; a side shut by hand stays shut.
- The Separator's outline and collision follow its flask, stand and fittings instead of a full block.
- Essences are redrawn as proper pixel art: crisp edges and a few clean shades, like Minecraft's own icons, for the
  essence items and for the essence signs in screens, over the crucible and on jar stickers.
- The Pocky Mods creative tab lists hextale's items in groups: research, the workshop, the altar, essence works,
  chemtech, pylons, automation, tools and armour, materials, ores, building blocks, nature, then essences, brews and
  research notes, with the creative-only items at the very end.
- The researcher's clothes are one step: pick the hood, robe, trousers or boots on its page for the recipe and runes.
- Hexsteel Gear shows the recipe of every piece of the set.
- The Hollowbloom moved into the Void's branch.
- A breach waits longer: the world tears only where the land has worn thin all the way. The haze, the miasma and the
  blight look and spread as before.
- The Hexcore as an item shows only its rune matrix, full size and sharp instead of a squeezed copy of the whole block,
  and its layers twist like a puzzle cube right in the slot. It is now the icon of the Pocky Mods creative tab.
- The Greedy Funnel, the Experience Condenser and the Hex Sawmill no longer have a hextech crystal floating over them:
  experience and essence flow straight into the funnel's mouth, the condenser's crown and the sawmill's guard.
- The crystals of the Arc Spire and the Whirl Reaper float a little higher, clear of the model, with all their effects.
- Bubbles in the mod's liquids (axolotl tank, crucible, alembic, jars, lab flask...) are Minecraft's own round bubbles
  tinted by the liquid instead of little cubes; on a boiling crucible they swell and pop like vanilla bubbles.
- The Lab Bench is bigger and centred in inventory slots.

## [1.1.0] - 2026-10-25
The Void seeps in: magic wears the world thin around a researcher's home.

### Added
- The Thinning: work with magic wears the edge of the world thin, and the Void starts to seep in. Everyday work wears it
  a little at a time - boiling essence in an alembic (unknown things double), separating it, drawing research ink,
  finished rituals, charged crafts at the Hex Workbench, crystals woken by lightning - and carelessness much more:
  essence poured away (far less where stone drinks it), raw crystals bursting, broken rituals, burst lab flasks, burnt
  parchments, Miasma Vent fumes. It lives in the chunk, never heals by itself, and the land around catches a share of
  it ring by ring, so a researcher's home and its surroundings sicken slowly as the research goes on.
- Water in sick land darkens towards a violet murk; the blight never touches water.
- The world takes a good deal before anything shows; past that, haze: a lilac veil low over the ground, dark motes
  sinking into the earth, grass, leaves, vines and water losing their colour, stone, sand, gravel and earth turning a
  sick lilac grey, the fog leaning to violet.
- Blight: the land itself sickens in a few spreading foci - grass, dirt and sand turn to Void Soil, stone to Void Rock, the world's
  plants on them die back and the Void's own growth takes root: Void Coral, Void Roots and glowing Void Bulbs. Void Rock
  cuts into Void Rock Bricks with stairs, slab and wall.
- Void Mites crawl out of blighted land at night and in the dark. They do not bite: a mite latches onto you and takes a
  heart of your maximum health for as long as it holds on - more mites, more of your hearts turn dead and infected
  (never the last one). A potion of
  healing, drunk, splashed or lingering, makes them all let go. You can see them on you.
- Miasma: the veil thick and knee-high, dark vapours seeping up out of the ground, magenta glints, the fog closing in,
  the edges of sight darkening, and the land murmuring from somewhere around.
- Breaches of the Void: where the land has gone all the way, the world finally tears - on open ground near a player,
  never within a thousand blocks of another breach. First the omen: the ground shakes, a drone rises and the world's
  motes are drawn to one point; then the breach slowly breaks open. It never closes, it keeps sickening the land around
  it, and whatever touches it is hurt and thrown back.
- Players near a breach as it tears open watch it happen: the camera flies out to it, holds while it opens and the
  first voidling pushes out, and flies back (skip it with a key, or turn it off in the client config).
- Voidlings: the Void's own spawn, bigger and far worse than mites. The cells of a breach bulge, and one drops out
  curled up, unfolds and runs at you - it leaps and bites. A breach keeps a few of them around it.
- Hollowbloom: sprinkle hextech dust on a dead bush and it wakes up hungry - the thinner the world around it, the more
  alive it is. Dry twigs on clean land; it stirs long before the land shows anything, leafs out, blooms around a hollow
  black heart as the haze comes, and glows in full bloom when the blight is near. Plant one at home and watch it; a
  comparator beside it reads how thin the world is there. It has its own step in the researcher's guide.
- Void Chitin: voidlings, and now and then a void mite, leave shards of their shell when a player kills them. Kept for
  what will be made against the Void (a new Void branch of the guide shows where it leads).
- `/hextale void get|set|add|stage|breach|clear` for operators; config options to turn the Thinning off or change its rate.

## [1.0.0] - 2026-10-24
Hextale leaves beta.

### Added
- Hex Foundry: a 3x3x3 kiln of Fire Bricks (hollow inside, iron bars in the middle of the top) with the new Foundry
  Hatch in the middle of its front, woken with hextech dust after it is researched. Slow to wake, but once hot it melts
  all nine of its inputs at once, up to twice a furnace's speed in each - far less fuel per piece than furnaces. It burns
  furnace fuel or draws fire and magma essence from jars nearby (pipes into any brick work too); things dropped on its
  top fall in through the grate, and hoppers or pylons feed and empty it through any brick. The hatch takes four runes
  (Forge, Radiance, Eternal Flame, Bellows, Tempering, Smelting - grains of metal in molten stone - and Lens) and keeps
  them, with its contents, when the kiln is taken apart.
- Fire Bricks: fireclay (clay, sand and gravel) baked into pale kiln bricks, with stairs, slab and wall.
- Multiblocks in the research book and in JEI are a turning 3D model that builds itself layer by layer - each block
  drops onto its place, a frame of light marks the layer being laid, empty cells glint, then dust falls and a wave of
  light wakes it. The arrows under the model pick one layer to study; below it the blocks that layer (or the whole
  structure) takes, with counts. JEI has a new Multiblocks category (drag to turn, the wheel steps through layers).

### Changed
- Every multiblock now wakes to a pinch of hextech dust (one is spent; free in creative), with a glittering puff and a
  wave of light climbing the structure. The Big Essence Jar no longer takes a hextech crystal.
- Devices still being worked out are no longer shown on the research map.

## [1.0.0-beta.5] - 2026-10-23
### Added
- Field Ward: a magic stone post with a hextech eye. Within 16 blocks monsters no longer spawn by themselves and no
  farmland is trampled - a quiet base leaves the mob cap to your farms. Spawners and animals are not affected.
- Miasma Vent: a rusted chemtech grate that chokes the hostile creatures standing over it in sickly fumes. Cheap -
  it burns the unknown essence the separator leaves behind - but slow, and no player struck them, so there is no
  experience or rare loot. Researched at the Lab Bench, made at the Hex Workbench.
- Experience Condenser: a glass column in a brass frame. No experience orb appears around it - it drinks them all
  and holds the experience as a glowing liquid, up to about level 50; a Greedy Funnel nearby pours in too. Take a level
  with an empty hand (sneak for everything) or fill glass bottles with Bottles o' Enchanting; a comparator reads it.
- Rune Shackles: a nullstone block with a ring of runes on top, laid into a mob farm floor - monsters spawn on it
  like on any block. Up to four creatures standing on it hang still in its glyph - no wandering, no pathfinding, no
  pushing - so a crowded mob farm hardly costs the server anything, and they can still be struck. Feeds on shadow
  essence; four blocks from one altar ritual.
- Hex Arc Spire: a hexsteel spike wound with brass coils that strikes the hostile creatures around it with arcs of
  charge. The kills count as a player's, so they drop experience and rare loot; named creatures and bosses are spared.
  Next to a Greedy Funnel it holds back while the funnel is full. Feeds on spark essence and takes runes (Shift, Motor,
  Chain Lightning, Flash, Dam, Lens).
- Greedy Funnel: a nullstone bowl set on a chest. Whatever dies around it never reaches the ground: the drops go
  straight into the chest and the experience into its crystal, so mob farms stop piling up items and orbs. It feeds on
  decay essence (bones are full of it) and takes runes (Shift, Radiance, Spoils, Engraving, Dam, Lens).
- The Researcher's Guide is split into three parts of the game: the first steps in the workshop, the middle around the
  Hextech Altar and the end around the Hexcore. Done steps wear the metal of their part (brass, blued steel, white gold),
  and each part has its own heart on the map: the Research Table, the altar and the Hexcore with its twisting rune
  tiles. Many devices still to come were added, moved to where they belong or dropped.
- Rune Chalk: draws runes on the floor. Runes around a Hextech Altar make its rituals quicker and glow while it works.
- Nullstone (+ polished, bricks): made in the crucible from calcite. Near a working Hexcore it drinks its anger and
  fills up, its violet veins glowing brighter, then slowly lets it out again.
- Crystal Battery: a dark cube in a brass cage that stores hextech charge from crystals and feeds the Hex Workbenches
  around it; its core glows when charged and a comparator reads it. Researched at the table, made at the altar. Takes
  runes (Cache, Chain Lightning, Dam, Lens).
- Hexsteel tools and armour: a sword, pickaxe, axe, shovel, hoe and a full set of armour as strong as diamond,
  forged on a crafting table from hexsteel ingots once hexsteel is researched.
- Arcane Cloth: wool thrown into a crucible whose water holds magic and beast essence comes out as heavy, warm cloth.
- Researcher's clothes: the first clothes of the mod - a hood, a robe, trousers and boots, each a real 3D model instead
  of flat armour, and the items in the inventory are the same models. The hood is deep and pointed, with a brass badge
  and a hextech crystal over the brow. The robe has a high collar that frames the head, a short linen cape with
  shoulder drapes and a hextech clasp, a deep blue coat of arcane cloth with brass hems, a belt with a buckle, a pouch,
  a rolled scroll and a vial of essence, and coat tails that swing with every step. The trousers are thick wool with
  linen wraps, a thigh pouch and a vial; the boots are leather with a brass buckle and a turned-down cuff. A little
  sturdier than leather; the robe takes the edge off fire, lightning and blasts. Researched at the Research Table,
  sewn on the Hex Workbench.
- Each piece takes one rune at the Hextech Altar, chosen from its own four. Hood: Banishing (night vision in the
  dark), Radiance (more experience), Breath (longer breath under water), Purification (poison and weakness wash off).
  Robe: Mending (all the clothes mend themselves), Healing (a blow may start regeneration), Fireproofing (fire on the
  robe brings fire resistance), Mirror (a blow may glance back at the attacker). Trousers: Haste (faster), Windmill
  (higher jumps), Gravity (less knockback), Shift (step up whole blocks). Boots: Lift (softer landings), Pump (easier
  walking in water), Pollen (crops and saplings by your path shoot up), Quenching (hot floors burn less).
### Changed
- The Hextech Altar needs Rune Chalk, and the Hexcore ritual takes a Crystal Battery and Regenerative Slime instead of
  two brass blocks.
- The Void (a first look, no logic yet): breaches in the world through which the Void shows. A breach is made of
  square cells with a burning magenta outline and a glow around it - teeth along the edge, loose pieces hanging beside
  it, darker rectangles and black dead cells inside, smouldering seams between them. Look into one and you see a
  throat, not a flat picture: dark coils wind down it, glassy lilac ribbons glow deeper in, and at the bottom a magenta
  heart beats behind a net of black roots - from whichever side you look. Five shapes - a gate, an eye with a blinding
  heart, a narrow slit, a scattered cluster and a breach in the ground. It spreads open from its heart and shrinks back
  into it, breathes out dark smoke and draws motes of the world in. Near it the fog closes in and turns violet, and the
  edges of your sight darken in the rhythm of its heartbeat. Deep, layered sounds for the opening, the breath and the
  closing.
- Void Shard (creative only for now): right click opens the Void where you look; sneak + right click closes the one
  you look at, or picks the shape (any, gate, eye, slit, scatter, breach).

## [1.0.0-beta.4] - 2026-10-21
### Added
- Hextech Skypack: two brass tanks of air essence around a hextech gemstone, worn on the back like elytra. Jump, then
  jump again in the air: a take-off from the ground fires its jets for two seconds like a firework rocket, then it
  glides until the next take-off from the ground. Every take-off costs a little air. Refill it from Essence Jars: right
  click a jar with the pack, or sneak beside jars while wearing it. Researched at the Research Table and made at the
  Hextech Altar (gemstone, phantom membranes, hexsteel, brass pipes, leather). It shows on the back; during the
  take-off long plumes of hextech flame burst from both nozzles with a ring of light and a roar, glints running down
  them - drawn on the flyer, so they stay with you at any speed - then thin out and die. The jets cough when the air
  runs low.
- Six runes for the skypack (three at a time): Motor (faster), Bellows (a kick when the jets catch), Dam (take-offs
  that cost no air), Breath (softer walls and landings), Fireproofing (fire resistance in flight) and Lens.

### Changed
Every particle and light effect of the mod is new: hand-made, high-resolution sprites with their own motion, each
fitting what it shows.

- Essence is alive now: once in a while a drop of glowing liquid seeps out from under the lid of a filled Essence Jar
  and floats up slowly, wobbling and changing shape as it evaporates - in the colour of an essence inside. Push a jar
  and it spills a few drops. A Big Essence Jar breathes along its whole lid.
- Essence drawn from jars into machines (Research Table, Lab Bench, Whirl Reaper, Hex Sawmill) flows as a chain of those
  living drops along a soft channel of light, gathering over the jar's lid and sinking into the machine.
- New particles everywhere instead of the vanilla ones: drops that fall and splash, bubbles that wobble and burst, soft
  steam and smoke that swells as it rises, crackling hextech sparks, twinkling glints, tongues of coloured flame, and
  rings of light that rush out when a ritual ends, a block wakes, a lens is cut or a brew splashes.
- The crucible boils, bubbles and splashes in the colour of what is in it; the drain valve pours a real stream of drops
  in the colour of the essence it drains.
- Rune procs show glints and a ring of light in the rune's colour; the altar's motes, the steles' rune shards, the
  shimmer motes and the magic tree's falling leaves are redrawn.
- Whirl Reaper whirls carry puffs of air and glints up the funnel; pylon tendrils glint at their root and tip.

### Fixed
- The drain valve's drops showed a random colour: the colour did not survive the trip to the client.

## [1.0.0-beta.2] - 2026-10-20
### Added
Every hextech tool and every working device now takes runes, and most runes are gifts you feel while you work: luck,
free buffs, rare golden finds. A rune that works shows it with a puff of dust in its colour. Runes are still found as
scrolls in treasure chests, so every scroll you find now has more places to go.

- Hextech pickaxe, eight new runes: Haste (a chance of Haste while digging, Haste II at three stars), Fertility (drops
  as with Fortune, on top of the enchantment), Radiance (more experience from ores, now and then some from stone),
  Breath (a chance to be fed a little; under water every block gives breath back), Banishing (Night Vision while
  digging in the dark), Fireproofing (Fire Resistance the moment a block opens lava), Eternal Flame (in the dark a hex
  wisp may stay where the block was - a mote of cold light that lights the tunnel for good and goes out at a touch)
  and Mending (wear may be shrugged off and a point mended).
- Hextech axe, shovel and sword take up to four runes like the pickaxe; a grindstone takes them off.
  - Axe: Gravity (the whole tree comes down at one blow, its leaves too; sneak for one block; a log wall without
    leaves is never felled), Growth (a tree cut at its foot is planted again, from a sapling in your bag or with a
    chance out of nothing), Pollen (leaves as with Fortune), Corrosion (a blow may eat through armour: more damage
    and the armour wears), Haste, Mending, Lens.
  - Shovel: Cache (soil, sand and gravel may hide a treasure - bones, flint, nuggets, clay, an emerald, a pottery
    sherd, a diamond, even a rune scroll), Erosion (digs 3 x 3 of soil, 3 x 3 x 2 at three stars; never the stone
    among it), Fertility (grass and soil give seeds, bulbs and saplings), Haste, Mending, Lens.
  - Sword: Withering (a blow may heal you), Chain Lightning (a blow may jump on as lightning to the hostile creatures
    around), Spoils (more loot, as with Looting), Radiance (more experience), Bellows (a kill may give Strength),
    Flash (the first blow on an unhurt creature strikes harder), Engraving (a kill may leave the head of a zombie,
    skeleton, creeper or piglin), Mending, Lens.
- Whirl Reaper, three runes instead of two and six new ones: Fertility (a reaped crop may be bonemealed at once),
  Pollen (one or two more of the produce), Photosynthesis (now and then a golden carrot, a glistering melon slice or a
  golden apple), Spring (every harvest wets the farmland around and may quicken the plants next to it), Radiance
  (harvests give experience, gathered over the reaper) and Dam (a whirl may cost no wind).
- Hex Sawmill, three runes instead of two and six new ones: Growth (a planted sapling may grow a stage at once), Pollen
  (leaves as with Fortune), Blades (a log may give one more), Photosynthesis (a felled tree may leave a golden apple),
  Hush (bee nests and hives on a felled tree come down whole, bees and honey inside) and Dam (a tree may cost no wind).
- Hex Furnace takes up to three runes (it alone on the altar) and keeps them when broken: Forge (each ore may give one
  more ingot), Radiance (more experience), Eternal Flame (a melt may burn no fuel), Bellows (the longer it works
  without a pause, the faster it melts, up to three times), Reforging (iron, gold and copper tools and armour melt
  into ingots, not a nugget) and Lens.
- The guide shows the runes of the reaper, the sawmill, the furnace and the three tools under their recipes, and JEI
  lists them with their numbers per star.

Chemtech begins: the cheap, dirty answer to hextech - chemicals instead of crystals, a green flame, rust.

- Crucible brews (researched at the Research Table): a catalyst thrown into boiling water that holds its essences
  turns the water into doses of a brew, one kind at a time; a glass bottle takes a dose as a Chem Vial. Five brews:
  Combustible Mix (coal into fire and ash), Growth Tonic (bone meal into plant and sap), Coolant (a snowball into
  cold and ice), Glow Mix (glowstone dust into light and slime, over soul fire at least) and Acid (a fermented spider
  eye into venom and decay, only over a Chem Burner). Hotter brews say what heat they need.
- Thrown vials splash: the growth tonic makes the plants around shoot up and young animals grow up, the coolant puts
  fire out and freezes lava into obsidian, the glow mix leaves patches of cold green Chem Glow on the blocks around,
  the acid eats away the stone around (ores stay), rusts iron blocks, weathers copper and burns monsters. Dispensers
  throw them too. A vial of combustible mix burns in any furnace as long as a blaze rod.
- Chem Burner (researched, made at the Hex Workbench): a riveted iron stove with a green window, the hottest heat
  there is. It heats a crucible, an alembic, a separator (which then burns no blaze powder) or a Hex Furnace (which
  then needs no fuel) standing on it, and burns only while that machine has work. It feeds on vials of combustible mix
  (by hand or hoppers) or on any essence through pipes at its back and sides - the separator's dark unknown waste
  burns twice as long. Up to three runes: Eternal Flame (fuel may last twice as long), Steam (a brew boiled over it may
  give one more dose), Radiance (brews give experience), Fireproofing (players beside it keep Fire Resistance) and Lens.
- Chem Glass (glass dropped into water holding venom) and its panes, Rusted Plating (iron blocks splashed with acid)
  with stairs, slabs and riveted plating.
- The guide's chemtech branch has the brews, the glass, the burner and the plating in place of their sketches; JEI
  lists the brews.

Shimmer: a bright violet liquid boiled from cave flowers and the healing of an axolotl - strong fuel, a strong draught
with no price to pay (yet), and the first bridge from chemtech to hextech.

- Shimmerbloom grows in bloom caves deep underground: some stretches of caves glow violet from wall to wall, carpeted
  with Shimmer Moss and hung with blooms from the ceiling, and most caves have none at all. Bone meal makes a new bloom
  take root nearby; it goes into a flower pot. Shimmer Moss and its carpet glow faintly.
- Axolotl Tank (researched, made at the Hex Workbench): an axolotl brought in a bucket lives in it, swimming slowly
  round; fed fish (by hand or hopper) or a bucket of tropical fish, it sheds a lump of Regenerative Slime every 90
  seconds. Nothing is done to the animal, and an empty bucket takes it out again whole. Up to three runes: Spring (one
  more slime), Haste (sooner), Dam (a meal spared), Healing (Regeneration for players around), Radiance (experience)
  and Lens.
- Raw Shimmer: regenerative slime thrown into boiling water holding dream and light (from dissolved blooms), over soul
  fire or hotter. It burns in a Chem Burner twice as long as combustible mix, in a pink flame.
- Chem Still (researched, made at the Hex Workbench): a copper pot still over a Chem Burner refines two raw vials into
  one of Refined Shimmer; the boiler's sight glass and the receiver show the liquids. Up to three runes: Steam (a
  second vial), Haste, Radiance and Purification (now and then a Synthetic Crystal grows out of the purest drops).
- Refined Shimmer is drunk: a minute of Strength II, Speed, Resistance, Haste and Regeneration II, then two minutes
  before the next vial works - no downside. It is the longest-burning fuel there is.
- Pink Brazier (researched, made at the Hex Workbench): burns shimmer in a cold pink flame; every two seconds a few
  plants in its light grow a stage. Up to three runes: Shift (its light reaches further), Pollen (a plant may grow as
  if bone-mealed), Healing (Regeneration for players in its light), Eternal Flame (a vial may burn twice as long) and
  Lens.
- Synthetic Crystal (researched, made at the Hex Workbench from amethyst, hextech dust and refined shimmer): cheap
  grown hextech that the workbench burns for charge.
- The guide's shimmer branch has its real steps in place of the sketches; JEI and Jade show the new devices.

The Lab Bench: chemtech is no longer worked out on the parchment - it is found out in a flask.

- Lab Bench (made at the Hex Workbench): a two-block bench with a tiled top, a flask in a ring stand over a small
  burner, a rack of droppers and an open journal. A glass bottle on the stand is the flask; the droppers fill from the
  Essence Jars of pure essence within 5 blocks, and every drop runs from its jar into the flask.
- Every chemtech topic is worked out here: the Chem Burner, the Axolotl Tank, the Chem Still, the Pink Brazier, the
  Synthetic Crystal, the Magic Tree Sprout and every brew - Combustible Mix, Growth Tonic, Coolant, Glow Mix, Acid and
  Raw Shimmer each on its own. The Crucible brews only what you have worked out.
- The chemistry is the essences': every compound forms from its two parents, but only in its own heat zone on the
  thermometer (cold, cool, warm, hot, searing), and what forms heats the flask further. Pick a topic in the bench's
  journal, let drops fall, turn the flame and watch the liquid change colour, bubble and flash as compounds form. Make
  enough of the topic's compound, pure enough, put its sample on the bench and seal the flask: Research Notes come out.
- Side reactions steal what you add at the wrong heat or in the wrong order; gas and searing heat build pressure - vent
  it (some of everything leaves with the steam) or the flask bursts and everything in it is lost. A zone you have seen
  a compound form in is written into your journal and marked on the thermometer.
- Up to three runes: Dam (a drop may cost no essence), Tempering (pressure builds slower), Haste (reactions run
  faster), Radiance (experience for every sealed flask), Heat Eater (a flask about to burst may let its pressure out by

- Researcher's Goggles: every link around shows while worn; looking at a pylon shows its reach, pace, link range, its

- Built to be cheap: items move straight between inventories in one transaction (only the fetching is drawn), idle

**Farming: the Whirl Reaper**
- Whirl Reaper: a brass mast on a foot of whorled magic stone, three framed sails turning round it and a hextech
  crystal floating on top in a little whirl of its own. Researched at the Research Table and made at the altar
  (hextech crystal, feathers, an iron hoe, brass, polished magic stone; wind, plant and crystal essence). Set among
  the beds, it sends a whirl of wind to the nearest ripe plant within 4 blocks (3 up and down): the whirl leaves the
  crystal, flies over to the plant and stands on it as a turning funnel of light, crumbs of the plant swirling up it.
  - Wheat, carrots, potatoes, beetroot (and modded crops), nether wart, cocoa and sweet berries are reaped where
    they stand: they start again from a sprout, one seed stays in the ground, the harvest falls on their own block.
  - A melon or pumpkin on its stem is lifted off into the whirl, spun and broken open where it lay.
  - Sugar cane, cactus and bamboo three blocks tall are twisted off above the root; the pieces rise turning round
    the funnel and drop beside the root (a cactus would burn them on top of itself).
  - Every whirl drinks wind essence from a jar within 5 blocks (1 mB for four whirls; feathers boil into wind); the
    jar's stream runs into the crystal. Without wind it stands becalmed, its sails barely turning.
  - A Gathering Pylon nearby picks up the harvest. Runes at the altar: Shift (wider field), Motor (whirls more often),
    Lens; a broken reaper keeps them. Right click shows its field, pace and wind; Jade too.
  - Light on the server: it reads its field block by block only when it looks, and an idle reaper looks more and
    more rarely; the clients play each whirl from one small message.
- Whorled Magic Stone: polished magic stone with a carved spiral (stonecutter).
- Hex Sawmill: a dark oak stump with a hextech saw disc of blued steel turning in a slot under a brass guard, a
  crystal floating over it. Researched at the Research Table, made at the altar (hextech crystal, iron axe, dark oak
  logs, an oak sapling, brass; wind, wood and metal essence). It throws a glowing copy of its disc at the nearest grown
  tree within 6 blocks (3 up and down): the disc flies over, saws through the trunk in sawdust and sparks, and the whole
  tree tips over away from the mill, slowly and then faster, and breaks apart where it hits the ground. Every log and
  every leaf of it is taken; the drops (logs, saplings, sticks, apples) land on its stump and a sapling from them is
  planted where it stood - four on the four feet of a big tree when four dropped.
  - Only trees grown from the ground are felled: logs on dirt with grown leaves. Log walls, pillars and trees whose
    leaves were placed by hand are never touched; roots under the ground stay.
  - Each tree takes 2 mB of wind essence from a jar within 5 blocks (the jar's stream runs into the crystal); without
    wind it stands becalmed. Runes at the altar: Shift (farther trees), Motor (saws more often), Lens; a broken sawmill
    keeps them. Right click shows its grove, pace and wind; Jade too. Sounds: the disc whirring off, the bite into
    wood, the trunk creaking over, the crash and the rustle of leaves.
